How I work

Open the door first

Help that starts only after selection goes to those already ahead; sociology calls it the Matthew effect. So our office hours let anyone in, and we meet founders before they apply.

Test the method before adopting it

Before using OKRs for the 2026 cohort, I interviewed experienced startup practitioners to check the approach. Each team now sets its own goals at the start, then works through four validation workshops and recurring group consultations by field.

Ask the people it's for

In 2016 I co-founded Forget About Age, an intergenerational program. Before our two-day camp in June 2017, we ran a World Café with the senior-university members it was for. In the survey I ran afterward, they rated our student helpers 4.95/5 and the drama sessions we had pitched as our signature last, so the next format we planned centered on trained student hosts.

Build it with agents, and kill it if it's wrong

I build with Claude Code. 6 of my 10 repos have an AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md with rules for the agents. The dashboard's AGENTS.md fences them off (no schema changes, no new endpoints, no new UI libraries) and requires a browser check of every UI change: screenshots in light and dark mode, KPI text read back, console errors scanned. The dashboard and BeSci both have CI. When I realized BeSci's mistake was an untested premise, I shut it down and wrote up why. On the next project I set the bar before running anything.
